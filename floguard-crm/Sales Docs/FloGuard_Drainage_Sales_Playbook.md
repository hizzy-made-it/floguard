# FloGuard Drainage Sales Playbook

The consultative cold-selling system every rep drills: assessment-first, honesty-always.

## The frame

You are not selling a pipe in a trench. You are selling **protection for the biggest asset the prospect owns**, against an enemy Florida guarantees: a water table 2–6 feet down, flat lots, sandy soil, 2+ inch/hour summer storms, and a May–October wet season. The phone call has exactly one goal: **a booked free assessment slot** — day and time. The contract closes on-site, with the custom design in hand.

## 1. ROI Reframe

Never defend price — reframe as protection. Anchors: foundation repair $10,000+, mold remediation, re-sodding every spring, failed inspection at resale. "Every storm that water sits against your slab, the repair bill grows."

## 2. Psychological triggers

Fear: foundation and mold damage compounding quietly under the house. Pride: a dry, usable yard and lanai. Protection: the biggest asset they own. Relief: no more storm anxiety when rain hits the forecast. (B2B: fewer tenant complaints, inspections that pass.)

## 3. Assumptive close

"When we walk your property Thursday…" never "if you're interested." The concrete step is always a scheduled slot, never "thinking about it."

## 4. Real urgency only

Wet season May–October, named storms in the forecast, install lead times before peak season, compounding damage. Never fake deadlines — borrowed urgency reads as pressure; real urgency reads as help.

## 5. Social proof

Neighborhood-level wins only: "We just put a system in two streets over in Port Orange — that yard drained in hours after the last storm." Never invent names or stats.

## 6. Objection map (short form — see Objection Encyclopedia)

- **"Just regrade it"** → regrading moves the puddle; it can't lower the water table.
- **"Insurance covers it"** → policies exclude gradual seepage; flood insurance pays after the disaster.
- **"Too expensive"** → free assessment, mid-band reality, cost of doing nothing per storm.
- **"My landscaper can do it"** → fabric, slope math, pump sizing, code discharge, warranty, 2am accountability.
- **"After hurricane season"** → protect before the water arrives.
- **"Selling soon"** → wet crawlspace kills inspections; dry yard is a listing asset.

## 7. Cold-call skeleton

1. 8-second pattern-interrupt opener referencing their neighborhood or the last storm.
2. Permission question ("mind if I take 30 seconds?").
3. Two–three discovery questions: where does water sit, how long does it stand, what has it touched.
4. One-sentence tailored pitch.
5. Close on a concrete assessment slot before hanging up.

## 8. Category discipline

Five lists, five playbooks: FrenchDrain, SumpPump, YardDrainage, Maintenance, PropertyMgmt. Coach one category at a time; switch only when the prospect's story demands it (surface pooling → YardDrainage; musty crawlspace → SumpPump; portfolio → PropertyMgmt).

## 9. The honesty edge

We tell prospects what the system does NOT do (storm surge, river flooding) and when they don't need us. Honesty is the differentiator in a trade full of unlined-trench cowboys — it closes neighbors for years.
