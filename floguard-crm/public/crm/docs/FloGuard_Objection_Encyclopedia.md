# FloGuard Objection Encyclopedia

Every objection gets: **validate → reframe → proof → close the assessment.**

## "Just regrade it / add dirt"

Validate: "Regrading absolutely has its place — for pure surface pooling it can be the whole fix."
Reframe: "But it can't lower a water table that's two to six feet down. When the ground itself is saturated, new dirt just moves the puddle — usually closer to the house. Chronic groundwater needs subsurface collection — fabric, gravel, pipe — and pumped discharge."
Close: "The free assessment tells you which one your yard actually needs. If regrading solves it, we'll tell you that."

## "Insurance covers it"

"Homeowners policies exclude gradual seepage and groundwater damage — that's the fine print almost nobody reads until they file. Flood insurance pays after a disaster; it doesn't prevent the damage or the claims history. The system is what keeps you from ever making that call."

## "Too expensive / $10k is crazy"

"The assessment is free — get the real number for YOUR yard before deciding anything; most quotes land mid-band, not at the top. And run the other math with me: foundation repair starts around $10,000, mold remediation on top, re-sodding every spring… the yard is already charging you. You're just paying the water instead of the fix."

## "My landscaper / handyman can do it"

"He might dig a fine trench. Here's what kills cheap drains in Florida: no filter fabric in our sand — clogged in a season. Wrong slope — water sits in the pipe. No pump sizing — flat lot, nowhere to go. Discharge dumped at the neighbor's slab — code problem. And when it fails at 2am in a tropical storm, who answers? We engineer it, warranty it, and answer."

## "I'll wait until after hurricane season"

"That's like buying the umbrella in November. Dry-season installs are easier on your yard, schedules are open, and you're protected before the water shows up — not cleaning up after it."

## "We're selling soon anyway"

"Then this matters more, not less. A wet crawlspace or standing water on inspection day costs you the sale or the price. A dry, engineered yard with a transferable story is a listing asset."

## "It's been fine for years"

"Florida water tables don't send warnings — neighborhoods change as development changes runoff. If it's started standing longer than it used to, that's the trend line. The assessment is free; worst case, you get confirmation you're fine."

## "I need to talk to my spouse"

"Of course — and the assessment is the thing worth deciding on together, because it's free and it produces the actual number. Most couples want the facts first. What day works for both of you to walk it with me?"

## B2B: "The owners approve all spend"

"Exactly why we start with one free pilot assessment and a written scope you can forward — real numbers, professional format, their call. You look prepared; nobody commits to anything."

## Do-not-call / hard no

"Understood — I'll take you off the list. If the yard ever changes its mind after a storm, we're in Port Orange." Log it. Never call again.
