# How a FloGuard System Works — Rep Explainer

Learn this cold. Homeowners buy from the rep who can explain the water.

## Why Florida homes flood from below

- **High water table:** often just 2–6 feet below the surface; during the May–October wet season it rises.
- **Flat lots:** gravity drainage needs slope; most Central Florida lots don't have enough.
- **Sandy soil:** drains fast but lets fine sediment migrate — unlined trenches clog within seasons.
- **Rain intensity:** summer thunderstorms and tropical systems drop 2+ inches per hour.
- **Power outages:** hurricanes cut power exactly when groundwater peaks — hence battery backup.

## The water path (teach it in this order)

1. **Surface water** — rain and runoff collect in problem areas around the home.
2. **Gravel trench** — filter-fabric-lined trench with clean gravel intercepts water before it reaches the foundation.
3. **Perforated pipe** — 4" pipe in the trench carries water by gravity toward the sump pit.
4. **Sump basin** — waterproof basin at the lowest point; float switch triggers the submersible pump.
5. **Safe discharge** — pump pushes water 10–20+ feet from the foundation to a swale, storm drain, or approved location. Check valve stops backflow; battery backup covers outages.

## What it protects against

Groundwater seepage, heavy rain soaking the soil, minor yard flooding near the house, musty crawlspaces and mold conditions, hydrostatic pressure against the slab.

## What it does NOT protect against — say this out loud

Major hurricane storm surge, river or street flooding above the slab or crawlspace openings, rainfall so prolonged it overwhelms pump capacity. **We say this on every call. Honesty is the brand.**

## Maintenance facts

- Homeowner: pour-test the pump monthly.
- Professional service yearly — ideally late spring before the wet season.
- Battery backup replaced every 2–3 years.
- Keep the discharge area clear; watch for roots and sediment (Florida's warm wet soil grows both).

We dig, line, pipe, backfill, and restore turf and beds. Most residential perimeter systems install in 1–3 days. When we leave, the system is invisible — the only thing you notice is that the yard is finally dry.
