The Condensed Playbook

The core consultative cold-selling system your AI Coach teaches, drills, and grades against — adapted for FloGuard drainage systems across Central Florida.

1The ROI Reframe

Never defend the price — reframe the expense as protection. A drainage system isn't a cost; it's what stands between Florida's water table and the biggest asset they own. Anchor to what water is already costing them: foundation repair runs $10,000+, mold remediation, re-sodding the yard every spring, a failed inspection when they sell. "Every storm that water sits against your slab, the repair bill grows. What did the last one cost you?"

2Psychological Triggers

Sell the feeling, then the engineering: fear of foundation and mold damage quietly compounding under the house, pride in a dry usable yard and lanai, protection of their largest investment, and relief from storm anxiety every time rain hits the forecast. For property managers: fewer tenant complaints and a portfolio that passes inspections.

3The Assumptive Close

Talk as if the assessment is happening: "When we walk your property Thursday…" not "If you're interested…". Close on a concrete step, never on "thinking about it": for homeowners that step is a booked free assessment slot — day and time; for property managers it's a pilot walk-through of their worst property.

4Urgency That's Real

Use true, local urgency — the May–October wet season, a named storm in the forecast, install lead times before peak season, and damage that compounds with every storm. Never invent fake deadlines; borrowed urgency reads as pressure, real urgency reads as help.

5Social Proof

Name real neighborhood wins as they accumulate: "We just put a system in two streets over in Port Orange — that yard drained in hours after the last storm." Neighborhood proof beats national stats every time.

6Objection Handling

"Just regrade it" → regrading moves the puddle; it can't lower a 2–6 ft water table. "Insurance covers it" → homeowners policies exclude gradual seepage; flood insurance pays after the disaster, it doesn't prevent it. Price → the assessment is free, most quotes land mid-band, and what does doing nothing cost per storm? "My landscaper can do it" → filter fabric, slope math, pump sizing, code-compliant discharge, warranty — and who answers at 2am? "After hurricane season" → protect the house before the water shows up, not after.

7The Cold-Call Skeleton

Eight-second pattern-interrupt opener referencing their neighborhood or the last storm. Permission question ("mind if I take 30 seconds?"). Two or three discovery questions — where does water sit, how long does it stand, what has it touched. One-sentence tailored pitch. Close on a concrete free assessment slot before you hang up.

8What You're Selling

Exterior French Drains: fabric-lined gravel trench + 4" perforated pipe, complete French drain + sump systems typically $4,500–$12,000 — quoted only after the free on-site assessment, never firm by phone. Interior Drains + Sump Pumps: sealed basin, float switch, check valve, battery backup for hurricane season. Yard Drainage & Grading: catch basins, channel drains, swales, downspout tie-ins — the lower-ticket entry offer. Pump Maintenance & Monitoring: seasonal storm-readiness plans, including systems FloGuard didn't install. Deposit, financing, and commission terms: [CONFIRM WITH OWNER] before quoting.

9Property Mgmt leads

PropertyMgmt is the B2B lane: property managers, HOAs, and landlords with portfolios of rentals. The pitch is fewer tenant complaints, one vendor, scheduled seasonal service, and clean invoicing. The close is always a free pilot assessment of their worst property plus a written scope they can hand the owner. Expect an approval chain and sell to it — never around it.
